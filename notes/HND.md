## What is solid

- **Battalion 3-16**: "at least 184" disappeared/killed is firmly documented — the
  Honduran National Commissioner for Human Rights figure, cited in U.S. congressional
  correspondence (1996). Treated everywhere as a floor. Led by Gen. Gustavo Alvarez
  Martinez; School of the Americas + CIA + Argentine ("Operation Charly") training.
  Sources: Wikipedia Battalion 3-16; HRW 1994 "The Facts Speak for Themselves";
  CJA Honduras program / Reyes v. Lopez Grijalba.
- **Conquest collapse dates/figures**: pre-conquest ~500,000 (Wikipedia: "nearer
  500,000," up to 2M) to ~800,000 (Spanish-conquest article, highlands); ~15,000
  natives under Spanish control by 1539, ~8,000 by 1541; "as much as 95%" decline
  in secondary summaries. Linda Newson, *The Cost of Conquest* (1986) is the
  standard scholarly monograph (confirmed bibliographic record on ResearchGate).
- **Football War casualties**: official Honduras 99 dead / 165 casualties; disputed
  upward (Nueva Ocotepeque priest ~400; CIA docs up to ~1,500); combined two-country
  estimates "several thousand"; ~300,000 displaced. Sources: Wikipedia Football War;
  Britannica.

## What is contested / NOT verified

- **Conquest death toll**: entered as null (low+high). The figures are a *population
  decline* range, not a death count — and exported slaves who died abroad, disease
  deaths, and battle deaths cannot be cleanly separated. A single death range would
  be fabrication. Event held UNVERIFIED. Could not obtain Newson's exact quantitative
  figures directly (book + the one located review are paywalled — Cambridge Core PDF
  returned only bibliographic metadata, no review text); numbers used are from
  corroborating reference sources, with Newson cited as the authoritative treatment.
- **1969 anti-Salvadoran pogrom death toll**: null. Sources say "dozens" killed and
  tens of thousands expelled (El Salvador: ~12,000 in days after 2nd match; up to
  ~300,000 over time; other displacement estimates 60,000-130,000). No source gives a
  verified count of those *killed* (vs. expelled), so toll held null, event UNVERIFIED.
  El Salvador formally charged Honduras with "genocide" and broke relations 26 Jun 1969.
- **Football War toll**: kept as a wide disputed range (99-3,000) precisely because
  official, clerical, and CIA figures diverge by an order of magnitude.

## Recorded in the country note but NOT entered as an event

Post-2009-coup violence: Bajo Aguan campesino killings and the murder of land/
environmental defenders (Berta Caceres, 2016). Real and ongoing, but the documented
case figures measure a contested, still-unfolding pattern rather than one closed
event with a settled toll — HRW documented 29 homicides in Bajo Aguan (none
convicted; private guards implicated in 13); the National Human Rights Commissioner
counted 92 killed in Bajo Aguan land disputes 2009-2012. No invented total entered;
folded into the note instead. (Sources consulted: HRW 2014 "No Justice for Wave of
Killings Over Land"; CCR; OAS/IACHR materials.)

### Source falsification results (all attempts to falsify FAILED → sources real)

- **hnd-1 (Spanish conquest, STR).**
  - Wikipedia *Spanish conquest of Honduras* — re-fetched; quotes verbatim: "estimated
    population of 800,000" in western/central regions; conquistadors "gained immediate
    income by selling natives into slavery on the Caribbean Islands and in Panama, and

    Lempira's revolt c.1537. CONFIRMED.
  - Wikipedia *History of Honduras (to 1838)* — re-fetched; quotes verbatim: "Estimates
    range up to 2 million, although the actual figure was probably nearer to 500,000";
    "In 1539 an estimated 15,000 Native Americans remained under Spanish control; two
    years later, there were only 8,000." CONFIRMED.
  - Newson, *The Cost of Conquest* (Dellplain Latin American Studies no.20, Westview,
    1986) — book is REAL: confirmed via Smithsonian catalog, Routledge/Taylor&Francis,
    VitalSource, Goodreads, Amazon, AND two published academic reviews (Duke *Hispanic
    American Historical Review* 67:4, 1987; Cambridge Core *Revista de Historia
    Económica*). HAHR review confirms the book's thesis: indigenous population "fell by
    more than 90 percent," driven by Spanish economic activity, encomienda/mine labour,
    slavery and disease — directly SUPPORTS the STR tier and the ~90% figure. The
    ResearchGate URL 403s to automated fetch but the work is over-confirmed elsewhere;
    bibliographic claim stands. CONFIRMED.

- **hnd-2 (Football War, WAR).**
