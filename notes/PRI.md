# Puerto Rico (PRI) — research notes

Built from scratch 2026-06-19. No prior seed file. Real web research; sources listed below.

## Structure decided

- **Victim ledger (2):** Taíno collapse under Spanish colonization; Ponce massacre 1937.
- **Perpetrator ledger (1):** the 1950 Nationalist-uprising repression (Utuado massacre + Jayuya bombing).

Puerto Rico has never been sovereign — Spanish colony 1493–1898, US territory since. So the
record is dominated by violence DONE TO its people. The perpetrator ledger is thin because it
is genuinely thin, not under-researched: there is no Puerto Rican army that fought abroad and
no Puerto Rico-organized mass killing of another people.

## Why the 1950 events sit in the perpetrator ledger

The decisive fact: the Utuado executions and the aerial bombing of Jayuya/Utuado were carried
out by the **Puerto Rico National Guard** (Maj. Gen. Luis R. Esteves) and the **Puerto Rico
Air National Guard**, mobilized by **Governor Luis Muñoz Marín** — the first popularly elected
Puerto Rican governor, who declared martial law. This is the one clear case where a Puerto
Rican government and Puerto Rican-led forces held the guns and turned them on Puerto Ricans
(the independence movement). It is double-edged: also a victim story (independentistas killed
for seeking sovereignty, inside a US colonial frame). I named the agency plainly and kept the
colonial context in the note/relevance. Marked **UNVERIFIED** at event level because casualty
figures are police estimates and the government suppressed news of the bombing.

## What is contested / could not be fully verified

- **Taíno pre-contact population of Borikén.** Sharply disputed. Common scholarly band
  ~30,000–60,000; some sources ~100,000; others up to ~600,000. This mirrors the wider
  Greater Antilles debate (Rosenblat low vs. Cook/Borah/Las Casas high). Used 30,000–60,000
  as low/high and flagged the dispute. These are population-DECLINE estimates, NOT a counted
  death toll — toll.note says so explicitly. Census anchors used: ~5,500 (1511 forced-labor
  census), ~1,545 (1531). The Livi-Bacci "Decline of the Tainos" PDF (UConn) would not extract
  cleanly, so I did NOT cite it — avoided citing a source I couldn't read.
- **1950 toll.** ~28 across all sites (≈16 Nationalists, 8 police/soldiers, 4 civilians) per
  police estimates; Utuado executions 4 killed outright + others left for dead. Internal
  discrepancies between the local Utuado count and island-wide totals are noted on the source
  pages themselves. Low=16 (Nationalist dead), high=28 (all sides).
- **Ponce massacre.** Solid: standard 19 dead (17 civilians, 1 policeman, 1 Guardsman by
  friendly fire), 200+ wounded; ACLU/Hays Commission. Low=17 (civilians), high=19.

## Events considered and NOT given their own entry

- **Río Piedras massacre (1935):** 4 Nationalists killed by Insular Police. Real, but smaller
  and same colonial-repression pattern as Ponce; folded conceptually into the victim arc.
- **African slavery in Puerto Rico (to abolition 1873):** ~30,000 enslaved at abolition (~5%
  of population). Considered as a perpetrator candidate (criollo/hacendado elite as agent),
  but the controlling agent was the Spanish crown/colonial system and no sourceable death
  toll exists for slavery as a mass-death event on the island; left out rather than invent a
  toll. The 1950 government repression is the cleaner, better-documented perpetrator case.
- **Later criminal massacres (Dupont Plaza arson 1986, etc.):** criminal violence, not state
  atrocity — out of scope.
