# Bangladesh (BGD) — research notes

Built from scratch on 2026-06-18 via live web research (WebSearch + WebFetch, mostly Wikipedia plus Amnesty and a New Internationalist feature). Four events, two per ledger.

## Victim ledger ("Done to them")

### bgd-1 — The 1971 genocide (status: UNVERIFIED)
- Perpetrators: Pakistan Army (Operation Searchlight, 25 Mar 1971), Razakar / Al-Badr / Al-Shams militias.
- Toll is the central problem: spans ~300,000 to 3,000,000 — an order of magnitude.
  - Bangladesh official figure: 3 million (national/memorial framing — genocidebangladesh.org).
  - Independent scholars: commonly 300,000–500,000.
  - Rummel: ~1.5 million.
  - Sarmila Bose (controversial low outlier): 50,000–100,000.
  - Hamoodur Rahman Commission (Pakistani official, widely rejected): 26,000 civilian casualties.
  - US CIA/State midpoint: ~200,000.
- Also: 200,000–400,000 women raped; ~10 million refugees fled to India (mostly Hindu), with large excess deaths in the camps (one cited figure ~562,915 excess refugee deaths).
- Marked UNVERIFIED because the body count is genuinely unresolved and politicised. Displayed range set 300,000 (low scholarly band) to 3,000,000 (official). Low source: Rummel + independent-scholar band. High source: Genocide Bangladesh Archive (advocacy — caveated).

### bgd-2 — Bengal famine of 1943 (status: RESEARCHED)
- Agent: British colonial administration + Churchill's wartime cabinet (boat/rice denial in the coastal east, refusal to divert imports), compounding a regional rice shortfall.
- Toll: range 800,000–3.8 million; scholarly consensus ~2.1 million (Cormac Ó Gráda).
- Classed STR: starvation then malaria/cholera/disease aggravated by malnutrition and displacement; man-made by policy but intent debated, so not EXT.
- Confirmed it hit East Bengal / modern Bangladesh hardest (Chittagong, Noakhali) under the "boat denial" policy — so it correctly sits in BGD's victim ledger as a death of the Bengali people, distinct from GBR's perpetrator entry.
- This is the most settled toll of the four (consensus figure exists), hence RESEARCHED rather than UNVERIFIED.

## Perpetrator ledger ("In their name")

### bgd-3 — Massacres of Biharis and non-Bengalis, 1971–72 (status: UNVERIFIED)
- Agents: Bengali nationalist forces, Mukti Bahini, Bengali civilian mobs.
- Toll wildly contested: ~1,000 (Minorities at Risk, low) to ~150,000 (Rummel, "prudent figure"); Bengali sources admit 30,000–40,000; international range 20,000–200,000.
- Named incidents: Santahar massacre (Mar 1971, ~300 killed); Khulna jute mill massacres (Mar 1971); 1972 Khulna massacres (Mar 1972, "thousands").
- ~250,000 Biharis remain stranded/stateless ("Stranded Pakistanis"); ~178,000 repatriated 1973–93.
- Classed POL (ethnic reprisal killing). Gerlach notes the historiographical move of using Bihari wartime collaboration to minimise the reprisals. UNVERIFIED due to the order-of-magnitude toll dispute.

### bgd-4 — Chittagong Hill Tracts campaign against the Jumma, 1977–97 (status: UNVERIFIED)
- Agents: Bangladesh Army, Ansar paramilitary, border guards + state-sponsored Bengali settlers.
- First-phase (1977–97) civilian deaths ~3,500–25,000 (indigenous + Bengali combined); ~30,000 total deaths cited; 6,000+ combatants; ~80,000 displaced.
- Demographic engineering: indigenous share of CHT population fell from ~98% (1971) to ~50% (2000) via the settlement programme.
- Massacres: Logang (10 Apr 1992) — 400+ dead by indigenous accounts vs 12 by government (army removed corpses); Naniarchar (17 Nov 1993) — at least 29 killed.
- 1997 peace accord ended the war formally but is largely unimplemented; killings never tried in court → received_lens "denied".
- Classed POL; human-rights bodies (Genocide Watch, Survival International) have used "genocidal," which I noted but did not adopt as the tier.

## What is contested / could NOT be verified
- The 1971 genocide toll: irreducibly disputed; I did not pick a "true" number, only a sourced low and high.
- The anti-Bihari toll: equally disputed; Pakistani and Bengali sources frame it in opposite directions.
- CHT per-massacre tolls: most never independently investigated; Logang spread (12 vs 400+) is itself evidence of suppression.
- Britannica's famine page returned HTTP 403 and was not used; famine figures rest on the Wikipedia summary + the Ó Gráda consensus surfaced in search and New Internationalist.
- No primary-document sources cited directly; the strongest non-tertiary sources used were Amnesty International (Logang) and the New Internationalist / Mukerjee for famine causation. Rummel and Gerlach cited from standard scholarly works without a live URL (url: null), per the no-fabricated-URL rule.
