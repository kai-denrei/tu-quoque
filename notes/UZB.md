## The asymmetry (why 3 victim / 1 perpetrator)
For almost the whole modern era Uzbekistan was NOT a sovereign actor — it was conquered by the Russian Empire (1860s–70s), swept through the Russian Civil War, then made a Soviet republic. So its victim ledger is deep and its sovereign-perpetrator ledger is thin. The big state crimes that happened on Uzbek soil (collectivisation, the 1937–38 Great Terror that gutted the Uzbek national/Jadid elite, cotton-monoculture coercion) were ordered from Moscow and belong primarily on RUS's perpetrator ledger; they are Uzbekistan's victim history. A genuinely sovereign Uzbek perpetrator ledger only opens at independence (1991), and its clearest entry is Andijan 2005. I did NOT invent a perpetrator event to balance.

## Victim events
1. **Russian conquest of the Uzbek khanates (1865–1876)** — colonial / WAR. Tashkent 1865, Jizzakh 1866, Samarkand 1868; Bukhara & Khiva reduced to protectorates, Kokand annexed 1876. Battle figures (Jizzakh ~6,000 killed; 1866 campaign >12,000 local dead) come from a tertiary aggregation of 19th-c Russian accounts that inflated enemy dead — kept as indicative only. No defensible aggregate, so toll.low/high = null, status UNVERIFIED.
2. **Turkestan famine (1917–1920)** — modern / STR (demographic collapse). Grain link to Russia severed by 1917; requisitioning + civil war deepened it. Toll genuinely contested: ~400,000 (≈19%) demographic low; Ryskulov ~one-third (~750k); **Marco Buttino ~1 million**. Region-wide for Russian Turkestan (overlaps Kazakh famine 1919–22), so not cleanly attributable to modern Uzbekistan. Low/high = 400k–1M, disputed, UNVERIFIED.
3. **Destruction of the Kokand Autonomy (Feb 1918)** — modern / POL. Tashkent Soviet + Red Army + Armenian Dashnak auxiliaries stormed and sacked Kokand, ending the first Central Asian self-government after ~78 days; fed the Basmachi insurgency. Toll ~14,000 commonly cited, "over 10,000" in some accounts, up to ~25,000 ("pogrom") at the high end. Low/high = 10k–25k, disputed, UNVERIFIED.

## Perpetrator event
4. **Andijan massacre (13 May 2005)** — contemporary / POL. Uzbek security forces (SNB, Interior troops, 17th air-assault brigade) under Karimov fired on unarmed protesters, removed bodies overnight, refused an independent inquiry. Toll deliberately obscured and sharply disputed: government 173–187; **HRW "hundreds" (~300–400 at the worst incident)**; a body-collector ~500 (per HRW); Free Peasants opposition 745; SNB defector Ikrom Yakubov alleged 1,500 (2008). Low/high = 173–1,500, disputed, RESEARCHED.

## Contested / could not verify
- **Uzbekistan 1933 collectivisation famine**: real but milder than Kazakhstan/Ukraine; no clean Uzbek-specific excess-death figure found. The UCA Becker/Li working paper (WP73) returned as a binary/encoded PDF via WebFetch and could not be parsed for an Uzbek number — NOT ledgered to avoid an unsourced toll.
- **State-run forced-labour cotton harvest (Karimov era)**: massive, well-documented coercion of millions (HRW reports 2012–2017) but not a mass-death event with a toll — noted in the country "note" rather than ledgered as an atrocity.
- **Yomud Turkmen massacre (1873)**: a real atrocity of the Khiva campaign but the victims were Turkmen — belongs on TKM's ledger, not Uzbekistan's. Excluded here.
- **Great Terror 1937–38 in Uzbekistan**: real and severe (decimated the Uzbek elite) but no clean Uzbek-specific toll located and the agent is the Moscow-directed Soviet state → belongs on RUS perpetrator ledger; left for the country note.
- Kokand 1918 and the Russian-conquest battle figures: no audited counts exist; ranges/nulls used deliberately.

### Source integrity — what was checked and found
- **uzb-1 (Russian conquest):** Wikipedia 'Russian conquest of Central Asia' and 'Battle of Jizzakh' both exist and support their claims verbatim — Jizzakh 18 Oct 1866 ~6,000 killed / 3,000 captured vs 6 Russian killed + 98 wounded; 1866 campaign >12,000 local dead vs ~500 Russian; chronology (Tashkent 1865, Samarkand 1868, Kokand 1876) and commanders (Chernyayev, von Kaufman) confirmed. Morrison, *The Russian Conquest of Central Asia* (CUP 2020/2021 reissue), ch. 6 'War with Bukhara, 1866–8' confirmed a real book/chapter. Colonial-source bias is disclosed in the caveat. Toll.low/high honestly null (no defensible aggregate); battle figures sourced as orders of magnitude. → VERIFIED.
- **uzb-2 (Turkestan famine):** Buttino, 'Study of the economic crisis and depopulation in Turkestan, 1917–1920', *Central Asian Survey* 9:4 (1990), pp. 59–74 confirmed real; the ~1M high anchor is in fact conservative (Buttino gives ~2M / 27% lost 1916–20). **Citation mismatch found and CORRECTED:** the original credited the Hamilton/Keller page with the ~19%/~400,000 and Ryskulov one-third low figures, but that page carries ONLY Buttino's ~1M. The low figures (400,000–750,000; Ryskulov ~one-third) are genuinely sourceable and were re-attributed to Wikipedia 'Kazakh famine of 1919–1922' (verified); the Keller URL retained for its Buttino corroboration only. Both ends now correctly sourced. → VERIFIED.
- **uzb-3 (Kokand Autonomy):** Toll bracket fully sourced and verified — 'over 10,000' (Wikipedia 'Turkestan Autonomy'), ~14,000 (Eurasianet, 'about 14,000 people dead'), 'as many as 25,000' pogrom (Wikipedia 'Basmachi movement'). Eurasianet article confirmed extant and re-tagged from supports:summary to a toll source (it carries the ~14,000 mid-figure). **QUARANTINE:** the 'Armenian Dashnak auxiliaries' attribution in the original agent + summary could NOT be verified in any cited neutral source — neither Wikipedia article mentions Dashnaks; the claim appears only in partisan Azerbaijani-state-aligned media (aze.media, news.az, karabakh.org) inside the Armenia–Azerbaijan information conflict, plus Soviet-era publications. It was REMOVED from agent and summary and a quarantine note added to toll.note. Remaining agent (Tashkent Soviet / Red Army under Osipov) is verified. → VERIFIED after correction.
- **uzb-4 (Andijan):** HRW '"Bullets Were Falling Like Rain"' confirmed verbatim — title, 'hundreds killed', 300–400 at worst incident, law-enforcement official's ~500-bodies account, government's 173 claim. Wikipedia 'Andijan massacre' confirmed government 187, foreign 400–600, 2008 Ikrom Yakubov 1,500 allegation, forces (17th air-assault brigade, Interior internal troops, NSS Special Forces). The Free Peasants / Ozod Dehqonlar 745 tally was not in the fetched section but is independently verified across multiple secondary sources. Both toll ends sourced; HRW bias disclosed. → VERIFIED.
