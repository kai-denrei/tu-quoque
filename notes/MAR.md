# Morocco (MAR) — research log

Built from scratch 2026-06-18 via live web research (WebSearch + WebFetch). No seed file existed.

## Ledger shape

- **Victim (1 event):** Rif War / Spanish chemical warfare (1921–1926).
- **Perpetrator (3 events):** Western Sahara invasion & occupation (1975–); Years of Lead state repression (1956–1999); Saadian conquest of Songhai (1591–1594).

The perpetrator side is the deeper one for Morocco, which is unusual and worth noting: the strongest, best-documented mass-atrocity events tied to Morocco are ones its state committed (Western Sahara, the Years of Lead) rather than ones done to its people. The Rif War is the one clean, large-scale victim event. I deliberately did NOT manufacture a second victim event to "balance" the ledgers.

## What I found (with the real sources consulted)

### mar-1 — Rif War (VICTIM), status UNVERIFIED
- Total war dead commonly cited at ~45,000–80,000 (all sides). Republic of the Rif: ~30,000 casualties incl. ~10,000 dead. Spain: 43,500–63,000 casualties; France 10,000–18,000. Battle of Annual (1921) alone: ~10,000–16,000 Spanish.
- **Key honest gap:** the ICRC's "The Rif War: A forgotten war?" states there is NO credible count of civilian deaths and that Rifian civilian losses were systematically underestimated or not counted. So the toll range is for all-sides totals; the Rifian-CIVILIAN figure and the chemical-weapons death count are both null.
- Spain used mustard, phosgene, diphosgene, chloropicrin from 1924 on civilian villages/markets/rivers — first sustained post-WWI aerial gas campaign on civilians. Mustard bombs sourced from German stockpiles, delivered via Melilla, dropped from Farman F60 aircraft.
- Later claims linking Rif cancer rates to the gas are NOT scientifically verified (ICRC notes comparable WWI-gassed regions in France/Belgium show normal rates) — excluded as a toll basis.
- Status UNVERIFIED because the civilian/gas death count — the figure most central to the victim claim — cannot be sourced; the all-sides range stands as toll.low/high.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Rif War"; ICRC International Review "The Rif War: A forgotten war?"; Wikipedia "Spanish use of chemical weapons in the Rif War".

### mar-2 — Western Sahara invasion & occupation (PERPETRATOR), status RESEARCHED
- Green March Nov 1975: ~350,000 civilians + ~20,000 troops. Morocco seized the territory as Spain withdrew.
- War (1975–1991) final toll sourced at 10,000–20,000 (all sides). >3,000 Sahrawis killed (Eckhardt 1985). Displacement: 40,000 (1976) -> 80,000 (1977).
- Early-1976 napalm/white-phosphorus bombing of refugee camps (Umm Dreiga, Tifariti, Amgala) is the signature civilian atrocity, but NO verified death count for the camp bombings specifically — so not stated as a number.
- Disappearances: ~520 still disappeared as of 2010; estimates 853+ (Project Disappeared) to ~1,500 (FIDH). >30,000 tortured claim made before UN bodies (advocacy claim, flagged).
- Strongest perpetrator event with a sourced toll range -> RESEARCHED.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Western Sahara War"; Wikipedia "Human rights in Western Sahara".

### mar-3 — Years of Lead (PERPETRATOR), status UNVERIFIED
- Hassan II's reign (here 1956–1999, chiefly 1960s–80s): enforced disappearance, secret detention, torture, extrajudicial killing.
- Tazmamart: 58 officers/soldiers disappeared after the 1971/1972 coups; 35 died over 18 years before the prison closed in 1991 (survivor-testimony based).
- Protest/riot deaths: Casablanca 1965 & 1981, Fes 1990 — "hundreds" but no firm count.
- **Firm floor:** Morocco's own Equity & Reconciliation Commission (IER, 2004–2005) examined ~20,000 cases and determined 742 disappeared individuals had died; 4,677 cases resolved, reparations to 3,657; 66 disappearance cases unresolved; ~US$85M paid.
- toll.low = 742 (state-confirmed dead). toll.high = null: no defensible upper bound across four decades of riot/detention deaths + unresolved disappearances. -> UNVERIFIED.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Equity and Reconciliation Commission"; Wikipedia "Years of Lead (Morocco)"; Wikipedia "Tazmamart".

### mar-4 — Saadian conquest of Songhai (PERPETRATOR), status UNVERIFIED
- 1590 al-Mansur sends 4,000–20,000-man gunpowder army across the Sahara under Judar Pasha. Battle of Tondibi (Mar 1591) routs the larger, firearm-less Songhai under Askia Ishaq II. Gao, Timbuktu, Djenné sacked. Timbuktu revolt (1593) suppressed harshly. 1594: scholars incl. Ahmad Baba deported to Marrakesh. Empire collapses permanently.
- **No sourceable death count** for the conquest, sacks, or suppressed revolts — both toll ends null. Act well attested, body count not. -> UNVERIFIED.
- Included because it is a genuine, well-documented Moroccan perpetrator event at the far (pre-colonial) end of the national story; left honestly null rather than padded with a number.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Moroccan invasion of the Songhai Empire"; BlackPast.org "Songhai Empire (ca. 1375–1591)".

## What could NOT be verified / deliberately excluded
- Rifian civilian death count and chemical-weapons-specific death count (no credible source — ICRC says so explicitly).
- Western Sahara refugee-camp-bombing death count (described as "thousands of civilians" but never quantified verifiably).
- Years of Lead total death toll (only the IER's 742 confirmed-dead floor is firm).
- Songhai conquest death toll (no contemporary or scholarly count exists).
- Cancer-rate-as-gas-toll claims for the Rif (scientifically unverified) — not used as any toll basis.
- Did NOT invent a second victim event to balance the ledgers; the asymmetry (1 victim, 3 perpetrator) is explained in the country note.
