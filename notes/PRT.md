# Portugal (PRT) — research log

Built from scratch, 2026-06-18. Status: RESEARCHED. 4 events (2 perpetrator, 2 victim).

## Shape of the ledger

Portugal is a heavily perpetrator-weighted entry, in scale comparable to Belgium's
(major colonial perpetrator, small victim side). The two perpetrator events dwarf
the two victim events in death toll. The asymmetry is structural — a maritime empire
that inflicted far more harm abroad than it suffered at home — and is explained in the
country `note`, not papered over.

## Perpetrator side

### prt-1 — Atlantic slave trade (1514–1866), STR
- Portugal invented and dominated the Atlantic slave trade; largest single carrier
  (~46% of the whole trade).
- SlaveVoyages / Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database: ~5.8M embarked, ~4.8M disembarked
  on Portuguese/Portuguese-Brazilian voyages → ~1M died in the Middle Passage. Brazil-route
  mortality ~12.5%. All-flag transit deaths >1.8M (SlaveVoyages), up to ~2M (~15%) per
  the Atlantic-slave-trade overview.
- **Toll choice:** I deliberately counted ONLY in-transit (Middle Passage) deaths —
  ~1M low / ~1.8M high — because the Atlas counts deaths, and the number of people
  enslaved (~4.8M survivors) is not a death toll. The note flags that capture/march/
  barracoon deaths before embarkation are uncounted and would raise the figure a lot.
- **Tier:** STR (structural mass death of an extractive system), not EXT — the intent
  was bondage-for-profit, not the destruction of a people. Matches the Atlas rule.
- **Contested:** carrier totals vary by cutoff date and by how post-1822 (independent
  Brazil) voyages are assigned. The TSTD "Portugal/Brazil" tabulation on the Slavery-in-
  Portugal page gives 5,467,957 across 1501–1850; SlaveVoyages carrier total ~5.8M.

### prt-2 — Colonial War + Wiriyamu (1961–1974), WAR
- Estado Novo's counter-insurgency in Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau.
- Civilian deaths across the three wars: ~70k–110k (Wikipedia infobox: 30–50k Angola,
  30–50k Mozambique, ~5k Guinea). I set the toll range at 50k–100k to stay conservative
  within that spread. Total war deaths incl. combatants exceed 100k.
- Wiriyamu massacre (16 Dec 1972): Red Cross ~150; Expresso (Felicia Cabrita, soldiers'
  testimony) ~300; named-victim reconstruction ~385–400. Operation Marosca planned by
  PIDE/DGS; agent Chico Kachavi told troops to "kill them all"; later assassinated.
  Exposed internationally July 1973 by Fr. Adrian Hastings on the front page of The Times.
- Documented Portuguese atrocities also include napalm bombing after the 1961 Baixa de
  Cassanje uprising (~400 dead, 20 villages), decapitations/impalings, chemical use in
  Angola, and forced resettlement into aldeamentos.
- **Scholarly anchor:** Mustafah Dhada, *The Portuguese Massacre of Wiriyamu in Colonial
  Mozambique, 1964–2013* (2016), reviewed in The English Historical Review.

## Victim side

### prt-3 — 1755 Lisbon earthquake, tsunami, fire, STR
- Natural catastrophe, no human agent. Largest mass-death event on Portuguese soil.
- Lisbon toll: ~12k (modern low) to 30k–40k; older contemporary accounts up to 60k
  (Britannica) and even 100k, which modern scholarship discounts.
- Alvaro Pereira: 40k–50k total across Portugal, Spain and Morocco (incl. ~10k Morocco).
- Toll set 12k low / 50k high to span modern-Lisbon-low to regional-total-high.
- **Contested:** record destruction; a later Nov 1755 tremor series complicates counting.

### prt-4 — Famine of the French invasions / scorched earth (1810–1811), WAR
- Peninsular War. French (Massena) advance on Lisbon + Anglo-Portuguese (Wellington)
  scorched-earth defence behind the Lines of Torres Vedras → ~40k–50k Portuguese dead
  of hunger/disease in the winter of 1810–1811.
- Note that the agency is dual: the invader's plunder AND the allies' deliberate
  wasteland. Tier WAR.
- **Contested:** an estimate of excess deaths for one campaign winter, not a count;
  sources give 40k or 50k.

## Could NOT verify / left out
- No single authoritative scholarly figure for "Portuguese-flag-only" Middle Passage
  deaths separate from the Portugal/Brazil carrier aggregate; used the database
  embarked-minus-disembarked difference (~1M) as the floor and the all-flag transit
  toll (~1.8M) as the documented ceiling for Portugal's share. Flagged in the toll note.
- Did NOT invent a precise Wiriyamu number; kept the sourced 150–400 spread in the note
  and used the war-wide civilian range for the headline toll.
- Earlier candidate victim events (Roman/Moorish conquests, Inquisition persecution of
  Portuguese Jews/conversos) were not included: the Inquisition is arguably a
  perpetrator-by-the-state event rather than "done to" the nation, and the conquest-era
  tolls are not cleanly sourceable. Left out rather than guess.

## Audit

Adversarial source-integrity + tier audit, 2026-06-18 (kainode). Premise: every
citation treated as fake until independently verified via WebSearch/WebFetch. All
four events resolved to **VERIFIED**; country status **VERIFIED**. No fabricated
sources were found — every author, work and URL checks out. Two citation-accuracy
corrections were made (mappings/wording), not falsifications.

### prt-1 — Atlantic slave trade (STR) — VERIFIED
- **SlaveVoyages (toll.low):** confirmed via search — ~5.85M embarked on
  Portuguese/Brazilian voyages (largest carrier), >1.8M all-flag Middle Passage
  deaths. The ~1M Portuguese-voyage transit-death floor is an honest
  embarked-minus-disembarked derivation, flagged as such. Real, supports the claim.
- **Atlantic slave trade Wikipedia (toll.high):** verified verbatim — ~12.75M
  embarked, up to ~2M died (~15-16%), Portugal named the major carrier. Both toll
  ends are therefore independently sourced.
- **Slavery in Portugal Wikipedia (summary):** source exists and tabulates the TSTD
  Portugal/Brazil rows; the per-period rows sum to ~5,838,956 (matches the ~5.8M
  used). CORRECTION: the previously cited exact aggregate "5,467,957" and the
  explicit "~46% share" were NOT found on the page in the fetched version — the
  article lists the rows without computing a total or percentage. Cite reworded to
  what the page actually shows; the ~46% share now lives only in the toll.note as a
  characterisation, and Portugal-as-largest-carrier is corroborated by multiple
  independent sources.
- **Tier:** STR re-derived and upheld — disease/conditions-driven structural mass
  death of an extractive system, intent was bondage-for-profit not annihilation.
  Correctly NOT EXT. STR-vs-EXT rule satisfied.

### prt-2 — Colonial War + Wiriyamu (WAR) — VERIFIED
- **Portuguese Colonial War Wikipedia (toll.low):** verified verbatim — 70k-110k
  civilian deaths, 30-50k Angola, 30-50k Mozambique, ~5k Guinea, napalm at Baixa de
  Cassanje (~400 dead, 20 villages). Headline 50k-100k sits conservatively inside it.
- **Wiriyamu Massacre Wikipedia (toll.high):** verified — Red Cross ~150, Expresso
  (Felícia Cabrita) ~300, 385 named victims. Both Wiriyamu bounds sourced.
- **Dhada 2016 / EHR review (summary):** book and the exact-URL EHR review (reviewer
  Justin Pearce, 2016) both confirmed real; corroborated by AHR and H-Net reviews.
- **Tier:** WAR re-derived and upheld — counter-insurgency atrocities aimed at
  breaking guerrilla support, not exterminating a people. Not EXT.

### prt-3 — 1755 Lisbon earthquake (STR) — VERIFIED
- **1755 Lisbon earthquake Wikipedia (toll.low):** verified — 12k-40k Lisbon,
  Pereira's 40k-50k regional total (incl. ~10k Morocco).
- **Britannica (toll.high):** WebFetch 403-blocked, but search confirmed Britannica
  reports "~60,000 in Lisbon." Source exists and supports the high end.
- **Tier:** STR upheld — natural catastrophe, no human agent. Correct.

### prt-4 — Peninsular War famine (WAR) — VERIFIED with correction
- **Lines of Torres Vedras Wikipedia:** verified — the page states "By some
  estimates 40,000 died." CORRECTION: this was mislabelled supports=toll.high; the
  page actually supports the toll.LOW (40,000). Remapped to toll.low.
- **toll.high (50,000):** the ~50,000 ("about 50,000 died of hunger or disease,
  Oct 1810–Mar 1811") is documented in the Peninsular-War literature but is NOT on
  the listed pages; flagged in toll.note as a single-sourced soft upper bound. The
  general Peninsular War article (supports=summary) does not itself cover the famine
  toll — its caveat already says it is "narrative context, not a precise count," so
  acceptable. The famine event therefore rests primarily on the Torres Vedras 40k
  figure plus literature for the 50k ceiling; both are scholarly estimates, not counts.
- **Tier:** WAR upheld — civilian starvation produced by invader plunder + deliberate
  Anglo-Portuguese scorched earth; deaths driven by conduct of war.
