## Done to them (victim)

### nam-1 — Herero and Nama genocide (1904–1908) — EXT
- German Empire's colonial army under Lt. Gen. Lothar von Trotha. Widely called the first genocide of the 20th century.
- Toll (population-loss estimate, not a body count): Herero ~40,000–80,000 (≈80% of pre-war population); Nama ~10,000 (≈half). Combined band entered as ~50,000–90,000. German government itself uses ~100,000 total for these groups in the period.
- Mechanism: rout at Waterberg (Aug 1904) → von Trotha's Oct 1904 shoot-every-Herero order → survivors driven into Omaheke Desert (death by thirst/starvation) → concentration camps (Shark Island/Lüderitz, Swakopmund, Windhoek; closed 27 Jan 1908) where ~half of prisoners died.
- Contested: how much desert mortality reflects deliberate extermination policy vs environmental death after forced expulsion (scholars divide).
- Recognition: Germany acknowledged genocide 2015; 2021 deal = €1.1bn development aid but rejected legal liability; Herero/Nama descendants rejected it (not allowed to negotiate directly). Negotiations over a joint declaration continued into 2024–2025; Amnesty (Oct 2025) still calling for reparatory justice.

### nam-2 — Cassinga raid (4 May 1978) — WAR
- SADF, Operation Reindeer, apartheid government orders. Airborne attack on SWAPO camp in southern Angola.
- Toll contested by composition AND status:
  - Low: SWAPO Central Committee secret report — 582 dead, 400 wounded.
  - High: Angolan government report — 624 dead (incl. 167 women, 298 teenagers/children), 611 injured.
  - SADF losses minimal (~3–4 killed); ~150 Cuban troops also reportedly killed in support.
- Core dispute: refugee camp (SWAPO/Angola narrative) vs SWAPO/PLAN military facility (SADF, and SA Truth & Reconciliation Commission 1998). Many dead were female/teenage and not in uniform → civilian/combatant split unresolvable. UN delegation called the action "criminal in legal terms and savage in moral terms." 4 May = Cassinga Day in Namibia.

## In their name (perpetrator)

### nam-3 — SWAPO spy purge / Lubango dungeons (1980s) — POL
- SWAPO security under Solomon "Jesus" Hawala ("butcher of Lubango"), within the Sam Nujoma-led movement. In exile in Angola.
- Trigger: South African raids → leadership believed the movement was infiltrated → mass arrests of own cadres as alleged SA spies; torture into false confessions; many killed/disappeared in pits ("dungeons").
- Toll: Marion Wallace — "hundreds, and possibly thousands" detained and tortured. 1993 U.S. State Dept report — 154–256 detainees still unaccounted for (entered as low/high). ICRC tracing (Nov 1991–April 1993) found none of the missing alive. ~400 survivors released as independence neared in 1989. Figure bounds the disappeared/presumed-dead, not a confirmed body count.
- Aftermath: SWAPO-led government refused a truth/reconciliation commission ("forget-and-move-on"); Hawala made head of the new Namibian army in 1990. "Breaking the Wall of Silence" (BWS), led by former detainees (Samson Ndeikwila, Pauline Dempers), still seeking accountability. Siegfried Groth's "Namibia: The Wall of Silence" (1996) and survivor memoirs (Oiva Angula, "SWAPO Captive") document it.

## What could NOT be fully verified
- Exact SWAPO-camp death/disappearance count: only a bounded range exists (154–256 unaccounted for per 1993 US State Dept; "hundreds, possibly thousands" detained per Wallace). No full accounting was ever made; precise body count is unknowable. Toll kept as a sourced range with the caveat explicit.
- A widely repeated UNMD (UN Mission on Detainees) figure of "1,110 names / 115 dead / ~315 unaccounted for" appeared ONLY in AI-generated search summaries, not in a source I could open and attribute directly (HRW PDF returned corrupted/binary; Grokipedia 403; HRW HTML summary lacked the numbers). Deliberately NOT cited — used the attributable 1993 US State Dept 154–256 range instead.
- Herero/Nama toll is inherently an estimate (no census of the dead); reported as population-loss bands, flagged in the toll note.
