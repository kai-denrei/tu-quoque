## Victim ledger (the heavy, well-documented side)

### vut-1 — Post-contact demographic collapse (c.1830–1930), STR
- After European contact (sandalwood traders from the 1820s–30s, then missionaries/planters), introduced epidemic disease (dysentery, influenza, measles, whooping cough) plus social disruption and trader-introduced rifles caused catastrophic population decline across the New Hebrides.
- Aneityum used as the documented archetype: contact-era population estimated ~3,800 (1854 first informed estimate; Spriggs modelled ~1830 from intensive irrigated-taro agriculture) up to higher 9,000–20,000 estimates; fell to under 200 by 1930. A 2025 peer-reviewed modelling study (Shanks et al., Journal of Pacific History) put the decline at >96%.
- Toll is a population-DECLINE estimate, not a counted death toll. Low/high (3,600 / 19,800) bracket conservative vs high contact estimates for Aneityum alone — explicitly NOT a whole-archipelago count.
- Real scholars confirmed: Matthew Spriggs (archaeology/demography of Aneityum), Norma McArthur (depopulation studies 1967–81), G. Dennis Shanks (dysentery epidemic lethality, AJTMH 2016; and lead on the 2025 Aneityum paper).

### vut-2 — Pacific labour trade / "blackbirding" (1863–1904), STR
- Ni-Vanuatu taken (deception, coercion, kidnapping) to Queensland and Fiji plantations; extreme death rates from disease and harsh conditions.
- Queensland: >62,000 indenture contracts / ~50,000 individuals 1863–1904; >14,564 Islander deaths 1868–1906 (~30% death rate, highest of any immigrant group in colonial Australia); first-year mortality ~81/1,000; peaked 14.7% in 1884. Source: Prof. Clive Moore's analysis of Queensland government records (the leading scholar on the trade).
- New Hebrides (+ Torres/Banks groups) a principal source — ~30,400 registered workers to Queensland per Encyclopedia.com. Also ~23,000 Islanders to Fiji overall.
- Death figures are for ALL Queensland Islanders, not ni-Vanuatu specifically; ni-Vanuatu share is large but not separately tallied, so low/high kept narrow around the documented Queensland total.

## Perpetrator ledger — deliberately empty (the asymmetry)
- Pre-colonial Vanuatu was not a state but a mosaic of small chiefdoms and language groups. Endemic intertribal warfare and ritual cannibalism existed (Big Nambas / Small Nambas on Malekula; last reported cannibalism on Malekula 1969). But this is small-scale raiding by STATELESS societies — no ruling elite/state as agent, no sourceable mass-death toll. Does not meet the perpetrator-ledger threshold (major atrocity by a state/forces/ruling elites).
- Post-independence (1980–): no documented mass atrocity. US State Department human-rights reports (2017–2023) consistently record no government extrajudicial killings or disappearances.
- Only candidate — the 1980 Coconut War: the new Vanuatu government (PM Walter Lini), using Papua New Guinea's Kumul Force, suppressed Jimmy Stevens's secessionist "Republic/State of Vemerana" on Espiritu Santo. Death toll ~1–2 (Stevens's son shot at a PNG roadblock; Stevens then surrendered, later sentenced to 14 years). Rebels were mainly armed with bows and arrows; "little fighting." This is a state response to armed secession with a negligible toll — NOT a mass atrocity. Excluded rather than padded.

## Contested / uncertain
- Aneityum contact population: wide estimate range (3,800 → 9,000–20,000); all are estimates. Toll bracketed accordingly.
- The widely repeated claim that the New Hebrides fell from "1 million in 1800 to 45,000 in 1935" is NOT supported by demographic scholarship — treated as unreliable and EXCLUDED from numeric ranges. (Appeared in a couple of tertiary/blog sources; rejected.)
- Labour-trade total figures (40,000 / 62,000 / ~50,000) differ by counting method (contracts vs individuals vs registered workers) — noted in source caveats.
- ni-Vanuatu-specific (vs all-Queensland-Islanders) death count: not separately available.
