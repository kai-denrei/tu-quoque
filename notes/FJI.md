# Fiji (FJI) — research notes

Built from scratch (no seed). Web tools (WebSearch/WebFetch) loaded and used for real research.

## Ledger decisions

**Victim — fji-1: 1875 measles epidemic (STR, RESEARCHED).**
The deepest single mass death in Fijian history. Measles arrived weeks after Fiji's
December 1874 cession to Britain, carried home from Sydney by paramount chief Cakobau's
party aboard HMS Dido; no quarantine was imposed despite quarantine being practised
elsewhere in the empire. A grand assembly of all 69 high chiefs then dispersed the virus
across the archipelago. Feb–Jun 1875 it killed ~one-quarter to one-third of the indigenous
population and almost all the high chiefs.

**Perpetrator — fji-2: Bau–Rewa wars / Cakobau's wars of conquest, 1843–1855 (WAR, UNVERIFIED).**
The cleanest Fijian-authored mass-violence record: the war-chief Cakobau of Bau waged a
12-year hegemonic war against Rewa and rival confederations, burning towns and ritually
cannibalising the slain (a documented practice of the era's warfare). Decisive battle:
Kaba, April 1855, won with Tongan help.

## What is contested / could NOT be verified

- **Measles toll.** Range 30k–40k. The ~40,000 / one-third figure traces to demographer
  Norma McArthur (Island Populations of the Pacific, 1967, p.8, ~150,000 baseline); other
  scholarly syntheses give one-fifth-to-one-fourth (→ ~30k low). Both death count and
  baseline population are 19th-century European estimates. A modern reassessment
  ("Was there a devastating measles epidemic in Fiji in 1875?", surfaced on ResearchGate)
  questions whether it was as uniformly catastrophic as the canonical figure holds — flagged
  as disputed. Could not access McArthur's original volume (cited via secondary epidemiology
  literature) → that source URL left null per the no-invented-URL rule.
- **Bau–Rewa war total death toll.** No reliable aggregate exists → event toll bracketed on
  the single best-documented engagement (Battle of Kaba) only, status UNVERIFIED. Kaba's
  own figures are internally inconsistent across sources: ~200 killed + ~200 captured at the
  low end, up to ~2,000 killed at the high end (the 2,000 figure is poorly sourced). Body
  count for ritual cannibalism is unestablishable.
- **PubMed article (11611508)** on British colonial administration during the epidemic:
  blocked by reCAPTCHA, could not extract; its existence/title corroborated via search index
  but not used as a numeric source.
- **Britannica Fiji history** and the Measles & Rubella Partnership page: both returned
  HTTP 403, not used.

## Deliberate asymmetry (explained in country note, NOT balanced with invention)

The two largest engines of mass suffering on Fijian soil after cession — **blackbirding**
(the Pacific labour trade; est. ~45,000 Melanesian Islanders taken to Fiji over ~46 years,
~a quarter dying under contract) and the **Indian indenture / girmit system** (1879–1916,
60,000+ labourers, brutal "coolie lines," highest indenture-colony suicide and infanticide
rates) — were perpetrated by the **British colonial state and European planters**, not by a
Fijian state (Fiji had no modern sovereign state until 1970). They belong in the
British/colonial perpetrator ledger, not Fiji's. The **1987 and 2000 coups** produced
ethnic intimidation of Indo-Fijians (Speight's 2000 coup) but only a handful of deaths —
no mass-atrocity toll — so they are summarised in the note rather than given an event.
This is why Fiji's perpetrator ledger is thin and pre-modern. Nothing was invented to fill it.

## Audit

Adversarial source-integrity + tier audit, 2026-06-19. Premise: every citation fake until proven otherwise. Country status RESEARCHED -> VERIFIED (both events VERIFIED).

### fji-1 — 1875 measles epidemic (STR) — RESEARCHED -> VERIFIED

Source integrity (all three sources falsification-tested and survived):
- **JMVH "Pacific Island Societies Destabilised by Infectious Diseases" (toll.low).** VERIFIED. Article fetched; states measles killed "an estimated one-fifth to one-fourth of the general population," Feb-Jun 1875, after Cakobau contracted measles in Australia and returned aboard HMS Dido, with the 69-chief assembly dispersing it and all/nearly all 69 chiefs dying. Supports the low bound and the narrative. Real, peer-reviewed, supports its specific claim. Bias (synthesis article, low end of range) disclosed in caveat. Clean.
- **McArthur, Island Populations of the Pacific (1967) (toll.high).** VERIFIED — and UPGRADED from secondary to PRIMARY. The full volume was obtained directly from the ANU Open Research repository (title page confirmed: "Island Populations of the Pacific / Norma McArthur / Australian National University"). Verbatim text found, quoting the 1893 Commission: "The people were estimated at that time to number about 150,000, and it is recorded, probably with fair exactitude, that 40,000 died from measles." The 40,000 figure is real and primary-sourced.
  - **CORRECTIONS MADE:** (1) The cite claimed "about one-third of ~150,000" — FALSE. McArthur herself states 40,000 is ~27 per cent (about one-QUARTER); one-third of 150,000 would be 50,000. Mischaracterization corrected throughout (cite, toll.note, toll.display, tier_reasoning, summary, and the country note). (2) The caveat said "URL null because consultation was via secondary citations, not the original volume" — now FALSE; primary text obtained, real URL added. (3) Discovered that McArthur's own island-by-island data give an average loss under 19 per cent and she suggests ~20 per cent may be truer than the 25-27 per cent customarily quoted — i.e. the "modern reassessment" doubt is partly internal to McArthur. Added to the note.
- **Fiji Times "Discovering Fiji | Bureiwai and the shadow of the measles epidemic" (summary).** VERIFIED. Article fetched; confirms HMS Dido, Sydney return, no quarantine, the chiefly cession assembly dispersing it to "every corner of the archipelago," Feb-Jun 1875, 30,000-40,000 dead ~a quarter of population, almost all 69 chiefs dead, and the indenture consequence. Supports the summary. Caveat (narrative-only, national framing) accurate.

Toll: both ends now independently sourced (low = JMVH one-fifth-to-one-fourth -> ~30k; high = McArthur primary 40,000). Disputed flag justified — the reassessment "Was there a devastating measles epidemic in Fiji in 1875?" is real (ResearchGate, academic re-analysis using later census data) and McArthur's own lower internal figure corroborates the dispute. Not single-source, not single-number.

Tier: re-derived BEFORE reading assigned tier. Introduced virus, no immunity, no perpetrator intent to destroy a people -> STR (disease-driven collapse). STR-vs-EXT enforced: this is NOT EXT. Assigned tier STR was correct. tier_reasoning tightened.

### fji-2 — Bau-Rewa wars / Cakobau's wars (WAR) — UNVERIFIED -> VERIFIED

Source integrity:
- **Battle of Kaba — Wikipedia (toll.high).** VERIFIED real. 1855 (7 April), Cakobau's Fiji-Tonga alliance vs Rewa/Bau under Ratu Mara; "Cakobau's men annihilated Mara's." Narrative: "200 of Mara's men were killed, and another 200 were captured." Infobox: 2,000 killed / 600 captured. Internal inconsistency is REAL and the high (2,000) figure is infobox-only with no in-text citation — confirmed by re-fetch. Caveat correctly flags this; retained only as upper bracket. Added an explicit audit note to toll.note recording that the high is weakly sourced and the low is double-sourced.
- **Fiji during the time of Cakobau — Wikipedia (summary).** VERIFIED real. Corroborates era inter-tribal warfare, Tanoa/Cakobau of Bau, Tongan involvement (Ma'afu from 1848), 1850s-60s consolidation. BUT it does NOT use the exact label "Bau-Rewa war 1843-1855" and does NOT name the Battle of Kaba — minor overclaim in the cite. Caveat tightened to say so explicitly; demoted to broad-chronology corroboration only.
- **Fiji Times "FEATURE | The Kaba wars" (summary).** VERIFIED. By Dr Subhash Appanna; covers Rewa's 1845 defeat, the failed 1853 Kaba assault, the decisive 7 April 1855 battle with Tongan King George's support vs Ratu Mara Kapaiwai, and gives "200 of Mara's men were killed, and another 200 were captured." Supports summary AND independently corroborates the low Kaba figure.

Toll: low (~200/~200 at Kaba) now double-sourced (Wikipedia narrative + Fiji Times). High (~2,000) single-source/infobox-only — flagged but kept as bracket. War aggregate genuinely null (no source supplies one) — honest null, not an omission. Single-number/single-source risk flagged on the high end only.

Tier: re-derived independently — hegemonic inter-polity war between Fijian chiefdoms, combat deaths -> WAR. Not EXT (no intent to destroy a group as such), not STR (not disease/famine). Assigned tier WAR correct. tier_reasoning expanded with the EXT/STR exclusion.
