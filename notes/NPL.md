# Nepal (NPL) — research notes

Built from scratch on 2026-06-18. Status: RESEARCHED.

## Structural finding: Nepal's atrocities are overwhelmingly internal

Nepal was never colonised, so there is essentially no record of large-scale mass
death inflicted by a foreign occupier. Its great catastrophes were Nepalis
killing Nepalis. This drives the deliberate asymmetry in the file:

- **1 victim event** (the civil-war civilian dead), framed from the standpoint
  of the slaughtered population.
- **3 perpetrator events** (Gorkha conquest of Kirtipur; the state's killings
  and disappearances; the Maoists' killings), separating out who did the
  killing.

The same 1996–2006 war therefore appears on both sides of the ledger — as
victimhood for the civilians (npl-1) and as perpetration for the two warring
Nepali parties (npl-3, npl-4). This is the cleanest dual-lens case in the
atlas where victim and perpetrator are the same nation in the same event. The
asymmetry is genuine, not a research gap; it is explained in the country note.

## Events

1. **npl-1 — civilian dead of the People's War (1996–2006), VICTIM, POL.**
   Toll ~13,000–17,800; ~82% civilians; ~1,300 missing. INSEC's ~13,256 is the
   most-cited count.
2. **npl-2 — mutilation of Kirtipur (1767), PERPETRATOR, WAR.** Atrocity of
   maiming, not killing — death toll deliberately left **null**. ~865 men/boys
   had noses and lips cut off (Daniel Wright's figure). Status UNVERIFIED.
3. **npl-3 — state killings & enforced disappearances (1996–2006), PERPETRATOR,
   POL.** Security forces ~64% of killings (~8,200); 1,234 of 1,619 NHRC
   disappearances attributed to the state. Doramba (Aug 2003) and Maharajgunj/
   Bhairabnath barracks (2003–04) as emblematic cases.
4. **npl-4 — Maoist killings & Madi bus bombing (1996–2006), PERPETRATOR, POL.**
   Maoists ~36% of killings (~4,500–5,000); Madi bus bombing 6 Jun 2005, 36–39
   civilians killed, the war's deadliest single attack.

## What is contested / could NOT be verified

- **Civil-war total toll.** Ranges ~13,000 (INSEC documented) to ~17,800 (broad
  counts incl. all combatants). Shown as a disputed range.
- **Per-perpetrator split.** The 64% / 36% security-force vs Maoist split is from
  INSEC data for 1996–2005; attribution is contested for a minority of cases.
  The ~8,200 and ~4,500–5,000 per-side figures are derived by applying those
  percentages, so they are estimates, not direct body counts — flagged in the
  toll notes.
- **Kirtipur (1767).** The death count is unknowable and was left null on
  principle (it was a mutilation, not a massacre). The number mutilated (865) and
  the very fact of the mutilation are **disputed**: Capuchin missionary
  eyewitness accounts and some Nepali sources record it; a notable share of
  modern Nepali commentary denies it. A separate ~1,200-dead manuscript figure
  refers to the years of fighting, NOT the post-surrender mutilation, and was
  kept out of the toll to avoid conflating the two. Event status UNVERIFIED.
- **Doramba toll.** Given as 19–21 across sources; used "about 19" per the NHRC
  finding.
- **Madi injured count.** Official ~52 vs victims'-committee ~75; noted as
  disputed.
- **OHCHR Maharajgunj report.** The OHCHR conflict-report page and the Maharajgunj
  investigation page returned HTTP 403 (access-restricted) to WebFetch. The UN
  findings were therefore sourced **via** Human Rights Watch's and Amnesty's
  summaries rather than quoted directly — flagged in the source caveat. Not
  fabricated; corroborated through secondary human-rights reporting.

## Items considered and excluded

- **Kot massacre (14 Sep 1846).** Real and pivotal (founded the Rana autocracy)
  but ~40 dead — an elite courtyard purge, too small for a mass-atrocity ledger.
  Could be added later as a contextual entry but does not meet the toll bar.
- **Rana-era autocracy (1846–1951).** A century of repression, but more
  political domination than discrete mass death; no clean sourceable toll.

## Audit

Adversarial source-integrity + tier audit, 2026-06-18. Standard: every citation
assumed FAKE until independently falsified; every source attacked; toll low AND
high required to be sourced; tiers re-derived from facts before reading the
assigned value. Verdict: **country VERIFIED — all 4 events VERIFIED, 0 flagged.**

### Source integrity — all 9 distinct sources verified, none falsified
- HRW, *Waiting for Justice* (2008) — REAL. Independently confirmed the exact
  figures it is cited for: INSEC ~13,256 / "estimated 13,000"; "over 8,000
  mostly civilian deaths after November 2001"; 1,619 disappearances reported to
  NHRC of which 1,234 attributed to security forces, 331 to CPN-M, 54
  unidentified; Nepal world-highest new WGEID disappearance cases 2003–04.
  Supports npl-1 toll.low and npl-3 toll.low as attached.
- Wikipedia, *Nepalese Civil War* — REAL. Confirmed 17,800 overall, 1,300
  missing, 13 Feb 1996 – 21 Nov 2006, infobox split (gov-force-attributed
  ~8,200; Maoist-attributed ~4,000–4,500), Badarmude 6 Jun 2005 as deadliest
  single attack (~38 killed, 70+ injured). Supports npl-1/npl-4 toll.high.
- Joshi & Pyakurel, *Individual-Level Data on the Victims of Nepal's Civil War,
  1996–2006*, International Interactions 41(3):601–619 (2015) — REAL. Authors
  verified: Madhav Joshi (Kroc Institute, Notre Dame) and Subodh Raj Pyakurel
  (chairman of INSEC). Census-not-sample dataset; supports the 82%-civilian /
  per-perpetrator attribution in npl-1.summary. (tandfonline page returns 403 to
  the fetcher = publisher bot-block, NOT non-existence; existence confirmed via
  RePEc, ResearchGate, and Joshi's CV.)
- Wikipedia, *Battle of Kirtipur* — REAL. Confirmed Shah's order to cut off
  noses and lips, Capuchin + "at least two Nepali sources," and Whelpton's exact
  judgement that the Capuchins had reason to dislike Shah but it is unlikely they
  fabricated the account. Supports npl-2.summary.
- Kathmandu Post, *What really happened* (18 Jan 2019) — REAL. Confirmed "865 in
  total according to Daniel Wright," the ~1,200-dead manuscript figure (Mathbar
  Singh Thapa, 1842, British Library) for the broader fighting, and ~75% of
  social-media respondents denying the mutilation. Supports npl-2 toll.high.
- Father Giuseppe (da Rovato), *An Account of the Kingdom of Nepal* (1786;
  translated by Sir John Shore in Asiatic Researches vol. 2, Calcutta 1790) —
  REAL primary Capuchin source; eyewitness line about mutilated defenders
  confirmed. null URL is correct for an 18th-c. primary. Supports npl-2.tier.
- Himalayan Times, *18 years on... Doramba* (18 Aug 2021) — REAL. Army "arrested
  and executed" detainees; the piece itself says 21. File uses "about 19" (the
  NHRC figure) and already flags the 19–21 spread — internally consistent.
- HRW, *Prosecute, Don't Promote* (22 Jul 2012) — REAL. Confirms the OHCHR May
  2006 report implicating the Bhairabnath Battalion (Maharajgunj barracks,
  Kathmandu) in ≥49 cases of enforced disappearance and torture, 2003–04.
  Supports npl-3.tier; the via-HRW routing is correctly disclosed in the caveat.
- Nepali Times, *Memories of terror in Madi* — REAL. Confirms 36 killed at
  Badarmude/Madi, victims civilians, official ~52 vs victims'-committee ~75
  injured. Supports npl-4 toll.low. (Prachanda's "biggest mistake" line and
  immediate apology independently corroborated.)

### Toll low/high coverage
- npl-1: low 13,000 (HRW) + high 17,800 (Wikipedia) — both ends sourced. OK.
- npl-2: low/high null on principle (mutilation, not killing); the mutilation
  figure 865 sourced (KP/Wright). Correct per law 3 — no invented death number.
- npl-3: low 1,234 disappearances (HRW exact) + high ~8,200 (HRW "over 8,000" AND
  Wikipedia infobox) — both ends sourced, high double-corroborated. OK.
- npl-4: low 36 Madi minimum (Nepali Times) + high ~5,000 Maoist share
  (Wikipedia, derived from INSEC 36%, flagged as estimate). Both ends sourced.
- No bare single-number / single-source contested toll found.

### Tier re-derivation (done before reading assigned tier; all CONFIRMED)
- npl-1 POL — political mass killing of a civilian population by state + insurgents,
  not destruction of a group as such (not EXT), not famine/disease (not STR). OK.
- npl-2 WAR — collective punishment / civilian-targeting violence inside a war of
  conquest; not extermination of the Newars as a group, not policy of a standing
  state, not disease. WAR is correct. OK.
- npl-3 POL — systematic extrajudicial killing + enforced disappearance as
  deliberate state repression; POL over WAR given the disappearance-programme
  character. OK.
- npl-4 POL — deliberate political killing of branded "enemies of the people" plus
  a civilian bombing; POL is defensible and symmetric with npl-3. OK.
- STR-vs-EXT trap N/A: no disease/famine-driven event in this set.
