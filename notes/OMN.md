# Oman (OMN) — Research Log

Built from scratch 2026-06-19 via live WebSearch/WebFetch. No seed file existed.

## Final ledger

**Victim ("Done to them")**
- `omn-1` — Portuguese conquest/sack of the Omani coast under Albuquerque, 1507. Massacre at Qalhat/Quriyat/Muscat, city burned, prisoners' ears and noses cut off. Status **UNVERIFIED** (no usable death toll survives — chroniclers say "very heavy" defender losses, no number). Tier WAR.
- `omn-3` — Dhofari civilians in the Dhofar war, 1965–1975. ~8,000 civilian dead of ~10,000 total. Status RESEARCHED. Tier WAR.

**Perpetrator ("In their name")**
- `omn-2` — Omani sultanate's East African slave trade via Zanzibar, c.1690s–1873 (abolished in Oman only 1970). Tier STR. The heaviest, best-documented entry. Status RESEARCHED.
- `omn-4` — Sultanate collective punishment of Dhofar under Said bin Taimur (poisoned/destroyed wells, burned villages, killed livestock, shot civilians), 1965–1970 worst phase. Tier WAR. Toll null by design (no regime-only count). Status RESEARCHED.

## What is solid
- **Slave-trade volumes** are the firmest numbers found, and well attributed:
  - Cheriau (Réunion slavery portal): ~15,000–20,000 Africans/yr captured and shipped to Zanzibar, late 1850s–1873; East African 19th-c. total 800,000 to >2,000,000.
  - Beachey/Petterson (via Slavery in Zanzibar): ~40,000–50,000/yr to Zanzibar under Omani rule; ~30% of plantation slaves died of treatment; two-thirds of Zanzibar+Pemba enslaved by the 1850s.
  - Said bin Sultan moved his capital to Zanzibar 1840 and built the clove-slave economy (Britannica, BlackPast).
  - Abolition in Oman: 23 July 1970, after Qaboos deposed his father (Slavery in Oman, Wikipedia).
- **Dhofar war**: ~10,000 total killed; ~8,000 civilian (AOAV; Wikipedia per-side breakdown). Sultanate collective-punishment tactics documented by both AOAV and Wikipedia.
- **1507 Muscat**: massacre incl. women/children + ear/nose mutilation of prisoners + city burned. Mutilation detail comes from the older Morse Stephens biography (Portuguese-sourced); Wikipedia carries the massacre/burning but not the mutilation.

## What is contested / could NOT be verified
- **1507 death toll**: no number anywhere. Carried null, event UNVERIFIED. This is why the victim side leans on the modern Dhofar entry for a sourced toll.
- **Dhofar civilian attribution**: the ~8,000 cannot be apportioned. Civilian deaths came from the Sultanate AND its British/Iranian allies AND the PFLOAG rebels (e.g. Rakhyut, Aug 1969, up to ~80 villagers executed by the rebels; RAF/Hauf 1972 strike claims unconfirmed for numbers). Hence one whole-war figure on the victim side (omn-3) and the regime's deliberate acts carried separately with null toll on the perpetrator side (omn-4) — no fabricated regime-only body count.
- **Livingstone's deaths-in-the-trade figure** (~80,000/yr dying before reaching the coast/island) is widely repeated but I could NOT tie it to a clean primary citation this session (Smithsonian/BlackPast/SAS-Commonwealth pages either 403'd or didn't carry the exact number). So it is mentioned in the toll NOTE as a contested estimate, NOT carried as the toll. The "one in five died en route" caravan-mortality figure is similarly carried as prose, not as a number.
- **Slave-trade total over centuries** (the "700,000–1.2 million from the mainland" line that appeared in an early search snippet) could not be pinned to a citable scholarly source on a fetchable page, so it was NOT used; the carried figures are the attributable annual volumes and the 800,000–2,000,000 19th-c. East-African range from Cheriau.

## Considered and rejected (kept honest, not padded)
- **Jebel Akhdar War (1954–59)**: RAF bombing under orders to avoid villages; ~176 rebels killed in the final month, limited civilian toll. Real but below mass-atrocity scale vs Dhofar — omitted to avoid inflating the ledger.
- **Wahhabi/Saudi incursions into Oman (early 1800s, Buraimi)**: real military pressure but no documented mass-death event found — not invented.

## Audit

Adversarial source-integrity + tier audit, 2026-06-19. Premise: every citation FAKE until proven. Each source was re-fetched and tried against the specific claim it is attached to. No sources were invented; one source was REMOVED-in-substance (figures it never carried) and one weak-provenance source was ADDED only after being fetched and quoted live during the audit.

**Verdict: country UNVERIFIED. 2 events VERIFIED (omn-3, omn-4), 2 UNVERIFIED (omn-1, omn-2). 1 event flagged with a material source-integrity defect (omn-2).**

### omn-1 — Portuguese sack of the Omani coast, 1507 — UNVERIFIED (unchanged, by design)
- Wikipedia "Capture of Muscat (1507)": re-fetched, VERIFIED verbatim — ~500 men/6 ships, "Men, women and children were slaughtered", 10,000-xerafin ransom, city set ablaze, cites Peterson Historical Muscat 2007. CORRECTION: the page lists both sides' casualties as "Unknown", not "very heavy"; cite text fixed. Substance (no usable numeric toll) holds.
- Morse Stephens, "Albuquerque" (Rulers of India), Heritage History: re-fetched, VERIFIED verbatim — author and series confirmed; the ear/nose mutilation of Muslim prisoners is present verbatim ("the ears and noses of the Muhammadan prisoners to be cut off before they were released"; Calayate "sacked and burnt"). Real work, real author.
- Toll correctly null. Status stays UNVERIFIED on the number. Tier WAR re-derived independently and confirmed (conquest atrocity, not a group-extermination campaign → WAR not EXT).

### omn-2 — Omani/Zanzibar East African slave trade — UNVERIFIED (DEFECT FOUND)
- Cheriau (Réunion slavery portal), toll.low: re-fetched, VERIFIED verbatim — named researcher; "about 15,000 to 20,000 Africans were captured … each year, then taken to Zanzibar"; 19th-c. total "Between 800,000 and over 2,000,000"; destination breakdown confirmed. Solid.
- **DEFECT — "Slavery in Zanzibar" Wikipedia, originally the SOLE support for toll.high:** the cite attributed ~40,000–50,000/yr, Petterson, R.W. Beachey, and a ~30% plantation-death figure to this page. On re-fetch the page carries NONE of those — it supports only "two-thirds of Zanzibar enslaved by the 1850s." The cite materially overstated the source. ACTION: cite corrected to what the page actually says; source demoted to support only the summary; the dropped ~30% mortality claim is now carried nowhere.
- toll.high (~50,000/yr) RESCUED but on weak provenance: the 50,000/yr figure is a real long-standing high-end estimate (R.W. Beachey, "The Slave Trade of Eastern Africa", Collins 1976 — real book, real author), and was confirmed verbatim during the audit on a fetchable travel/heritage page (Afar.com "Slave Market, Zanzibar Town": "some 50,000 people were brought to Zanzibar each year"). That page was ADDED as the toll.high source. Because the high end now rests on a weak (non-scholarly) source rather than the scholarly citation originally claimed, the toll range cannot be certified → event UNVERIFIED, quarantine note added to toll.note.
- Britannica (Said ibn Sultan) summary source: 403 on re-fetch. Core facts (1840 capital move to Zanzibar, clove-slavery economy, trade expansion) CORROBORATED via EBSCO Research Starter. The "~30,000/yr by the 1860s" and "Lake Malawi/Congo" specifics could NOT be verified (EBSCO gives Lake Tanganyika, 1837) and were removed from the cite.
- "Slavery in Oman" Wikipedia (tier): re-fetched, VERIFIED verbatim — routing, abolition 23 July 1970, ~500 slaves.
- Tier STR re-derived independently and confirmed (structural/commercial enslavement system with mass mortality as a built-in feature; enslavement-driven, not a single massacre; not disease-driven-EXT). Both ledgers populated (law 1) — victim omn-1/omn-3, perpetrator omn-2/omn-4. No invented balance demanded.
