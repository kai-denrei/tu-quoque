## mdg-1 — Repression of the Malagasy Uprising (1947–49), VICTIM, tier WAR

What happened: nationalist secret societies (Panama, Jiny) launched a revolt on 29 March 1947. The French army suppressed it over ~2 years with executions, torture, village burning, collective punishment, and "death flights" (prisoners thrown from aircraft). The MDRM party was dissolved and its deputies condemned.

Toll — heavily contested, and this is the crux:
- French administration claimed 89,000 (1947), then "over 100,000" (1949), then revised DOWN to 11,342 "known dead" (1950). The 11,342 figure is a colonial state-organ count, widely seen as an undercount.
- Jean Frémigacci (French colonial historian, L'Histoire no. 318, March 2007) argued the demographic curve cannot support 89,000 and estimated 30,000–40,000 total, of which only ~10,000 were violent deaths, the rest famine/disease among displaced populations — many driven into flight by the insurgents themselves. He explicitly said there was "no forgotten genocide," no intent to exterminate.
- Demographer Alfred Sauvy countered Frémigacci: a contemporaneous colonial anti-malaria campaign may have masked the population loss in the curve.
- I set low=11,342 (firm published floor), high=100,000 (upper official claim), disputed=true, with the full dispute in the toll note.

Tier reasoning: WAR not EXT. These are counter-insurgency war crimes during armed suppression; even the most toll-skeptical historian denies extermination intent. Tiering this EXT would be libel; ignoring it would whitewash.

received_lens: memorial. Madagascar commemorates 29 March as Martyrs' Day (declared a day of mourning by Tsiranana in 1967; national holiday). A national museum was inaugurated at Moramanga in 2012. Note: there was a long post-independence silence, partly due to diplomatic relations with France — but the dominant present framing is memorial.

## mdg-2 — The reign of Ranavalona I (1828–61), PERPETRATOR, tier POL

What happened: the Merina queen used lethal forced labour (fanompoana), the tangena poison ordeal to kill suspected enemies, executions of Christians and political rivals, and subjugation campaigns against non-Merina peoples.

Toll — contested and made of NON-COMPARABLE figures (must not be summed):
- Deliberate killing: W.E. Cummins (British missionary, 1878) counted 60–80 Christians executed; in 1849, the worst year, 18 executed and ~1,900 punished.
- Tangena ordeal: ~1,000 deaths/yr in the 1820s rising to ~3,000/yr under Ranavalona; one 1838 estimate put cumulative Imerina tangena deaths as high as 100,000 (~20% of the population). Source: Gwyn Campbell, "The State and Pre-Colonial Demographic History: The Case of Nineteenth-Century Madagascar," Journal of African History 32:3 (1991), 415–445.
- Military campaigns: Campbell estimates ~60,000 non-Merina killed in the campaigns of Radama + Ranavalona (1816–1853).
- The headline "5 million (1833) → 2.5 million (1839)" is a POPULATION-DECLINE figure, driven mainly by disease, malnutrition, slaving and forced-labour stress per Campbell — i.e. STR-type causes, NOT a death count of intentional killing. It is contested and should never be presented as an extermination toll.
- I set low=60 (firmest attested deliberate-execution count), high=100,000 (upper tangena estimate), disputed=true, full caveats in the note.

Tier reasoning: POL, not EXT and not STR for the headline act. The deliberate, attributable killing (tangena as terror tool, executions) is political terror = POL. The mass demographic collapse is STR-driven (disease/famine/forced labour) and is flagged in the toll note as such rather than counted as intent. Calling the whole thing EXT would misattribute disease deaths to genocidal intent.

received_lens: contested. The "mad queen" portrait is now widely regarded as a colonial/Victorian distortion (European-dominated, hostile sources). Historians Gwyn Campbell and Gerald Berg reframe her as a rational nationalist defending Malagasy sovereignty against European encroachment. Malagasy highland views today span the full spectrum tyrant↔nationalist — genuinely contested, not settled.

## What could NOT be fully verified

- Exact 1947 toll is genuinely unresolvable; the 11k–100k range is a real historiographical dispute, not a data gap. Left as disputed range.
- Tangena and population figures depend on 19th-century estimates filtered through Campbell's reconstruction; the underlying primary counts are inherently imprecise. The 100,000 / 20% 1838 figure is an old estimate, retained as the high end with caveat.
- I did not access the full text of Campbell (1991) or the L'Histoire article behind paywalls/originals; figures were confirmed via multiple secondary citations that consistently attribute them to those named works. No citation, author, or URL was invented.
