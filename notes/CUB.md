## Done to them (victim)

### cub-1 — Collapse of the Taíno of Cuba (1511–1550s), STR
- Spanish conquest under Diego Velázquez from 1511; encomienda forced labor, tribute, conquest violence, and Old World epidemics.
- Caribbean-wide: ~80–90% of the Taíno died within the first 30 years; up to 90% of the native population in the region within a half-century.
- Pre-contact population for Cuba specifically is genuinely unsettled — estimates run from tens of thousands to several hundred thousand. Used a deliberately wide bracket (50,000–300,000) flagged as a population-DECLINE figure, not a body count.
- Hatuey (cacique from Hispaniola) crossed to Cuba to organize resistance, was captured and burned at the stake — a concrete, well-attested detail.
- Tiered STR: disease-driven demographic collapse compounded by deliberate enslavement/violence, per the atlas rule.
- CONTESTED: disease-vs-violence weighting. Reséndez (The Other Slavery) argues enslavement was a major killer, not disease alone; cited via the Taíno-genocide reference overview, not his monograph directly.

### cub-2 — The reconcentración (1896–1898), POL
- General Valeriano Weyler's forced relocation of the rural population into garrison towns/camps to starve the independence rebellion of support. First orders Feb/Oct 1896; abolished March 1898 by Ramón Blanco.
- Often called the first modern concentration-camp system.
- Deaths overwhelmingly from starvation + dysentery/typhoid/disease in the camps.
- CONTESTED toll: low band ~155,000–170,000 (commonly cited as ~10% of population); high scholarly estimates up to 400,000. Bracketed 155,000–400,000, ~170,000 most common.
- Scholarly anchor: John Lawrence Tone, *War and Genocide in Cuba, 1895–1898* (2006) — URL is to a published review (Project MUSE), not the full text; noted in the caveat.

## In their name (perpetrator)

### cub-3 — Revolutionary firing squads and political killings (1959–present, core 1959–60s), POL
- Post-1959 revolutionary tribunals → firing-squad executions ("al paredón"), extrajudicial killings, deaths in custody under Castro's government.
- Che Guevara commanded La Cabaña Jan–June 1959; executions THERE estimated 55 (Jon Lee Anderson) to 105, with several hundred nationwide in the period.
- Cuba Archive (Free Society Project, Maria Werlau / the late Armando Lago) documents ~5,600 firing-squad executions, ~1,200 extrajudicial assassinations, ~10,723 total regime-attributed deaths; methodology requires two independent sources per case.
- Bracketed 5,600 (documented executions) – 10,723 (documented total).
- CAVEAT FLAGGED: both AEI (the cited outlet) and Cuba Archive are politically opposed to the regime; figures are documented minimums. The Cuban government and some scholars dispute the atrocity framing (wartime-justice / Nuremberg-style justification). Anderson's 55 used for toll.low as the lower-end scholarly figure.
- received_lens = denied (regime contests framing/responsibility).

### cub-4 — The '13 de Marzo' tugboat massacre (13 July 1994), POL
- State vessels (Polargo 2 and Polargo 5) rammed and sank a tugboat of ~72 fleeing civilians ~7 miles off Havana; water cannon used on people on deck.
- 41 dead including 10 children; 31 survivors. Toll NOT disputed (disputed:false).
- PRIMARY source: Inter-American Commission on Human Rights, Report No. 47/96, Case 11.436 (16 Oct 1996) — found Cuba responsible for violating the right to life of the 41. Verified the report number, date, and finding directly against the UMN Human Rights Library copy.
- Amnesty International (AMR 25/013/1997) characterizes deaths as extrajudicial execution.
- Castro called the agents' actions a "truly patriotic effort"; government claimed accident. received_lens = denied.

## Dual-lens framing
The same 1959 revolution that ended the Batista dictatorship — itself following a long arc of colonial (Spanish) and authoritarian brutality on the island — became, in power, a perpetrator against its own people (cub-3, cub-4). Cuba's victim ledger (Spanish conquest, Spanish reconcentración) and perpetrator ledger (the revolutionary state) are separated by the change of who holds power, not by the presence or absence of atrocity. Captured in the country note.

- Did NOT include Cuba's overseas military interventions (e.g., Angola) as a distinct perpetrator atrocity event — the documented mass-atrocity attribution there is weaker/more diffuse and better belongs to the relevant African-country files; avoided inventing a toll to balance.
